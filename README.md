# interstate-wage-gaps
This repository contains an R-based analysis investigating whether the U.S. geographic wage gap widened between 2004 and 2024 at both the state and county levels. Using payroll data from the BLS Quarterly Census of Employment and Wages (QCEW), the research evaluates percentile dispersion ratios (P90/P10) and real wage growth.  

Key findings include:
- Aggregate geographic wage inequality remained roughly stable across both state and county scales over the 20-year period.
- Despite overall stability, the geography of the wage distribution experienced massive churn, with extreme growth in shale-oil counties and slow growth in rural Appalachia and the Rust Belt.
- Recent geographic wage shifts are driven by industry-specific churn rather than a broad, uniform divergence of top-earning regions.
